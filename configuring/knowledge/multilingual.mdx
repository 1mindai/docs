## The one thing to remember

Superhumans are trained in **English only**. When a Superhuman speaks another
language, the underlying language model translates the English response into that
language **in real time**. There's no stored translated version, so there's
**nothing to manually edit at the translation level** — which is the reason behind
almost every "we can't do that."

## How it works today

- Training and the Brain are in English; there's no separate per-language training.
- Non-English output is generated on the fly by the model at conversation time.
- We don't keep a Dutch / German / French / etc. copy of the content to edit.
- Translation quality is tied to the model and improves as newer models ship.

**A helpful way to think about it:** English is the **master document** — change it
and it stays changed. Other languages are like a **live interpreter** reading that
document aloud each time: you can't edit the interpreter's phrasing (and it may
word things slightly differently each time), though you can hand them a short
glossary for specific terms (which is how brand names and acronyms are handled).

## What we can do

- **Number and currency pronunciation** in the target language (spoken words vs. reading out decimals, correct currency phrasing) — supported.
- **Language-specific CTA / links** (e.g., a Dutch free-trial link vs. the English one) — coming soon; confirm it's live before promising it to a customer.

## What we can't do (today)

- **Edit or correct a translation** — word choice, swapping a phrase, or changing tone/formality (e.g., forcing "Hola" over "Buenos días"). It happens at the model level, so there's nothing stored to edit.
- **Match a specific regional dialect on request** (e.g., Spain vs. Mexican Spanish) — under investigation.
- **Per-language training** — we won't build a separate Dutch/French/Spanish Superhuman.
- **Word-by-word translation corrections** — even where a one-off fix is technically possible, we don't support it (it adds latency, instability, and an open-ended queue).

## In progress

- Language-specific link/CTA overrides.
- Dialect-level control (passing regional dialect to the model) — no committed date.
- Newer models — many of today's rough edges resolve as models advance, with no manual work.

## How to set expectations with a customer

- "Translations are generated in real time by the underlying language model. We can't manually edit the wording, phrasing, or tone of a translation today — that's controlled at the model level, not configured word by word."
- "In English we're editing the actual source your assistant is built on, so we can fine-tune wording. Other languages are translated and spoken live — more like a real-time interpreter — so there's no fixed version to edit yet."
- "The models keep improving, and we're evaluating newer versions plus more regional/dialect control."
- Where we *can* help: number/currency formatting, and (soon) language-specific links.
